## 📌 Week Overview

**Week 10** is the culmination of the entire DACA programme. Instead of learning new technical skills, this week focuses on **synthesising everything** we have learned and **presenting our work** to the UrbanStyle board of directors. This is our chance to demonstrate business impact, communicate our findings clearly, and prove that we are ready to work as professional data analysts.

The week includes:
- Finalising the GitHub portfolio (READMEs, structure, AI usage fields)
- Preparing a **7-minute team presentation** for the board meeting
- Practising responses to Q&A using the STAR method
- Reflecting on our personal growth throughout the 10 weeks

## 🔍 My Individual Contributions

For the 45‑second relay segment, I presented my **city name standardisation** work from Week 1 and explained why it matters for business decisions.

### What I Did

- Showed the problem: unstandardised city names causing incorrect customer counts
- Demonstrated the solution: using `TRIM()` and `INITCAP()` to normalise data
- Explained the business impact: accurate city-level marketing and store location planning

### The Message

> “Without cleaning, we would have made decisions based on inaccurate data. My work ensured that UrbanStyle can trust its customer location data – leading to better targeting and more efficient resource allocation.”

## 💡 Key Learnings from the Entire Course

Reflecting on the whole 10‑week journey, I identified five core takeaways that define my growth as a data analyst:

1. **I learned how to build a dashboard with Python** – using **Streamlit** and **Plotly** to create interactive, shareable reports.

2. **I started using GitHub again** – after a long break, I re‑adopted version control and now maintain a professional portfolio.

3. **I learned about virtual environments** – understanding `venv` and dependency management is crucial for reproducible work.

4. **I integrated AI heavily into my programming** – using AI tools like ChatGPT for debugging, code generation, and explanation, while keeping the analytical logic my own.

5. **I realised the importance of well‑structured, commented code** – especially for teamwork, as clean code makes collaboration smoother and reduces misunderstandings.
