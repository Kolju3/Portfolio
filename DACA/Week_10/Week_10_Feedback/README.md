## 📌 Overview

This document captures my **personal reflections** on the entire DACA journey, with a focus on Week 10 – the final portfolio defense. It covers how I felt about the process, what I learned about myself, and how I plan to continue growing.

---

## 🎯 The Final Week – How I Felt

Week 10 was both **exhilarating and nerve‑wracking**. The pressure of presenting to the “board” (our mentors and peers) pushed me to:

- **Synthesise** 10 weeks of work into a clear, concise story
- **Practice** my delivery until it felt natural
- **Trust** the skills I had built

Despite the nerves, I felt a strong sense of **accomplishment** knowing I had a solid portfolio to back up my words.

---

## 🧠 What I Learned About Myself

### 1. I Can Learn Fast

Looking back at Week 1, I couldn’t even write a `SELECT` statement without looking up the syntax. Now I confidently write complex queries, build Python scripts, and design dashboards. This proves that with consistent effort, I can acquire new technical skills quickly.

### 2. I Enjoy the Business Side

I discovered that my favourite part of data analytics is **connecting technical work to business outcomes**. Cleaning city names feels abstract until you realise it helps marketing teams target the right cities. This motivates me to always ask “So what?” after every analysis.

### 3. I Need to Practice Public Speaking

While I am comfortable writing code and documenting my work, presenting to a group still makes me anxious. I need to practice **telling the story** of my projects more confidently and concisely.

### 4. I Value Teamwork

Working with Helen, Natalia, and Olga showed me that different perspectives make the analysis stronger. I learned to appreciate constructive feedback and to give it in a way that helps others improve.

## 💡 What I Would Do Differently

If I could repeat the course, I would:

1. **Start documenting earlier** – I left many of the `README.md` files until the end. Next time, I would write them as I go.

2. **Ask more “So what?” questions** – I sometimes got lost in the technical details. I would constantly ask myself: “Why does this matter to the business?”

3. **Practise presentations more** – I would record myself and review my performance to improve clarity and timing.

---

## 🌟 Final Thoughts

DACA has been a transformative experience. I came in expecting to learn technical skills, but I gained so much more: a portfolio, a network, a new mindset, and the confidence to pursue a career in data analytics.

I am grateful to the mentors, my team, and the entire DACA community for this opportunity. I am ready to take the next step.
